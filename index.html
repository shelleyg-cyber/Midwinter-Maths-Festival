Station 1 · Antarctica ❄️

Snowflake Symmetry

Every snowflake grows with six-fold rotational symmetry because of how water molecules lock together as they freeze. Build your own snowflake below — draw on one slice and watch maths copy it six times!

✏️ Activity: Draw a snowflake

Click and drag on the canvas. Your strokes are mirrored and rotated 6 times — that's symmetry at work.

⭐ Challenge question

A snowflake has 6-fold rotational symmetry. Through how many degrees can you rotate it so it looks exactly the same?

Station 2 · Fields of Europe 🌻

Nature's Numbers: The Fibonacci Spiral

Sunflowers, pinecones and shells grow following the Fibonacci sequence: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13… where each number is the sum of the two before it. Seeds packed at the "golden angle" (about 137.5°) fill space perfectly.

🌀 Activity: Grow a sunflower

Slide to add seeds and watch the spirals appear — count them and you'll find Fibonacci numbers!


⭐ Challenge question

The Fibonacci sequence starts 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13… What number comes next?

Station 3 · Cities of the World 🕰️

Time Zones: Maths Keeps the World on Time

Earth spins 360° each day, so it turns 15° every hour — that's why the world is divided into time zones. While we're rugged up for midwinter in Melbourne, it's midsummer in the north!

🌏 Activity: Live world clocks

These clocks tick in real time. How many hours is each city behind Melbourne right now?

⭐ Challenge question

Earth rotates 360° in 24 hours. How many degrees does it rotate in one hour?

Station 4 · The Alhambra, Spain 🔷

Tessellations: Art Made of Maths

For centuries, artists from Spain to Morocco have covered walls with tessellations — patterns of shapes that fit together with no gaps and no overlaps. Only some shapes can do it!
